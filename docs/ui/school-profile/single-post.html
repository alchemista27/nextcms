Annual Science Exhibition Brings Innovation to Light

calendar_today October 15, 2026 Sarah Jenkins schedule 5 min read

This year's Annual Science Exhibition at SMaRT School was a testament to the boundless creativity and intellectual drive of our students. Held over two days in the main auditorium, the event drew over 500 visitors including parents, industry professionals, and representatives from top universities.

From sophisticated robotics arms capable of precise manipulation to solar-powered water purification systems, the projects on display reflected weeks of dedicated research, prototyping, and iteration. Judges noted an exceptionally high standard across all grade levels.

Standout Projects

Among the most celebrated entries was "AquaClean," a solar-powered water filtration prototype developed by a team of Class XII students. The device is designed for rural communities and can purify up to 50 liters of contaminated water per day using only sunlight as its power source.

"We wanted to solve a real problem. Clean water shouldn't be a luxury — it should be a right. AquaClean is our small contribution to that goal." — Rizky Pratama, Team Leader

Another standout was a machine learning model trained to detect early signs of skin conditions using a smartphone camera. Built by Class XI students with guidance from Dr. Linda Park, the project won first prize in the Technology Innovation category.

Words from the Principal

Principal Dr. Budi Santoso expressed immense pride in the student body: "What I see in this exhibition is not just scientific knowledge — I see empathy, creativity, and a genuine desire to contribute to society. This is exactly the spirit we hope to nurture at SMaRT School."

  • First Prize Technology: "DermaScan AI" — Class XI
  • First Prize Environment: "AquaClean" — Class XII
  • Best Innovation Award: "Urban Vertical Farm Bot" — Class X
  • Most Creative: "BioLuminescent Signage" — Class X

The exhibition concluded with a keynote address from Dr. Anita Kusuma, a leading research scientist at LIPI, who encouraged students to pursue STEM careers and to never let setbacks derail their curiosity.
